> since when did empathic propaganda become illegal?

It hasn’t—**“empathic propaganda” isn’t a special category that suddenly became illegal.** The legal issue is usually about **what the speaker is** (government vs. private actors) and **what exactly they’re doing** (e.g., covert/targeted messaging or using certain funds), not about “empathy” itself.

A common source of the “propaganda became legal” claim is confusion around the **U.S. Smith–Mundt framework** (a set of rules tied to U.S. government-funded international broadcasting). In short:

- **Domestic dissemination by U.S. government outlets was restricted for decades**, because the concern was influencing U.S. public opinion with government media made for abroad.
- **In 2013**, a modernization law **lifted/changed parts of a long-standing ban on making certain government-produced content available to domestic audiences** (with conditions), mostly to reflect the internet era and practical accessibility. This did **not** mean “the government can target Americans with propaganda however it wants.” `<citation src="3"></citation>`  
- Separately, there are still restrictions discussed in the legal/advocacy literature about **targeting Americans** and **using appropriated funds in ways that qualify as impermissible “propaganda.”** `<citation src="5"></citation>`

> I don't understand what you're saying - why can youtube ban their channel?

YouTube can ban a channel for violating its **rules**, which are about conduct and content—not because “propaganda” is illegal in general.

Common reasons that lead to termination include things like:

- **Spam / deceptive practices:** manipulating viewers, misleading metadata/thumbnails, repeated reuploads, or coordinated “engagement bait.”
- **Inauthentic or coordinated behavior:** networks of accounts amplifying the same content to create an appearance of broad support.
- **Harmful or policy-prohibited content:** harassment, threats, incitement, or content that violates YouTube’s rules around safety and exploitation.
- **Copyright / reuse issues:** some channels get hit if they repeatedly repackage content in ways that violate copyright or platform reuse policies.
