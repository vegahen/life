As my defensive doctor kept reminding me, they had never received a single complaint; when I had finally started sleeping again after switching [epistemologies](/psychology/epistemology/epistemology.md), I booked a new appointment to renew my sick leave and told my doctor that I was not going to be their first complaint, but I would like to share the feedback that they might want to be more empathic in the future so they don't accidentally expose their patients to seventeen days of psychological sleep torture - I think my doctor must belong to the TikTok generation or something, because they just nodded and said uh-huh while staring at their screen doing something completely different.

What planet this person lives on is completely beyond me, how the fuck are you are able to helpfully conclude a conversation with maybe you're right, maybe you're wrong... about a mathematical statement equivalent to `2 + 2 = 5`... which is given as the reason for not providing sick leave that was objectively valid no matter how many hours I had actually worked... maybe you should try using a calculator next time since your calculations are so error-prone, because I heard that it is your strict duty to have thought through backdating and document why you're doing it...

I have since learned - I should have just taken the salary deduction and walked out, because sitting in front of a highly educated doctor, sleep deprived, with reduced brain capacity, doubting my own reality because the seemingly rational person right in front of me is completely immune to mathematics had some adverse psychological aftereffects on me.

## History

I cross-referenced with the hours I actually worked, as opposed to the hours I logged. Logging hours is a real headache when you need to switch between work breakdown structures all the time, so I prefer just summing up the minutes at the end of the week to make sure the right amount of time is charged to the right clients.

Wednesday the week prior, one and a half weeks following my concussion, was my first day without any headaches. I remember doing a deep clean of my car for a couple of hours and spending the rest of the day relaxing at home.

Thursday the week prior was my first day back at the office. I spent around six hours at the office including breaks, doing mostly mundane catch-up tasks since I had been gone for almost two weeks and attending a seminar for a couple of hours after `16:00`.

Friday the week prior was my second day back at the office. I spent around eight hours at the office, a few hours talking with clients. Other than that it was mostly breaks and socializing. 

On Monday I did actual work from `11:45 - 15:00` and `16:00 - 16:30`. My doctor called at `13:59`, and we had an uncomfortable conversation that lasted five minutes. My doctor wanted me to work more hours than I was comfortable with, even though I told them I was going to need more flexibility so that I could take breaks as needed. My condition worsened the following hour, which led to me taking a break at `15:00`. I tried to resume work at `16:00` to finish my tasks, but realized after half an hour that I needed to just go home.

On Tuesday I stayed home and attended a remote meeting from `09:30 - 09:45`, pushing through a major headache. I tried again during a meeting from `12:00 - 12:15`. I had a small hope that this was a temporary setback and that I just needed to sleep some more, but at the end of the day I was still completely incapable of looking at a screen.

I called the doctor's office on Wednesday at `09:24` and told them I needed a physical appointment with my doctor as I felt they were not taking my problems seriously over the phone. Every single interaction prior to the phone call that Monday had also left me feeling that there was something a bit off, but I wasn't able to put my finger on it until they straight up told me to work more hours than I was comfortable with.

I know several people who have struggled for years due to concussions and are still not fully back at work, and one of them sent me [this article](https://www.stolav.no/4a72b0/siteassets/avdelinger/rehabiliteringsklinikken/brosjyrer/langvarige-plager-etter-lette-hodeskader.pdf). It states that individual risk factors - such as mental health issues, post-traumatic stress, anxiety, and rumination - are more correlated with long-term aftereffects than the actual severity of the head injury.

I remember telling my doctor that I was in a high-risk group, but I don't remember if I only said that it was because I worked in IT and had several colleagues who struggled with it, or if I also mentioned my post-traumatic stress, rumination, and affinity for epistemological and philosophical dilemmas. I did mention those things after the whole sleep-torture ordeal was over, but they just responded in the same way they had done over my feedback about empathy. Then they gave me seven weeks of sick-leave and said a few things about Seroquel.

## Conclusion

I don't think concussions and mathematical gaslighting are a great combo when you have previous [traumatic experiences with gaslighting](/psychology/theories/general-theory-of-relativity.md) and happen to have a master's degree in physics and mathematics.

I have no idea how common it is to perform the sort of doublethink I'm seeing in the transcript above - am I the only one seeing it? But I'm having a really hard time imagining that any of the other doctors I have ever met in my life would not just have performed backdating the second I showed them the numbers on my calculator, saving themself three minutes of discussion and an entire appointment the next day.

But luckily it made me realize that all human beings are irrational, instead of just launching me into a spiral of hardcore paranoid-schizoid trauma phantasies against the public health services - I wonder if there are many people out there who chose to go down that road instead...

This is the last time I will ever trust what a doctor tells me without having performed a thorough evaluation of their cognitive abilities.
