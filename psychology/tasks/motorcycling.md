One of my closest near-death experiences was when I almost shared my fate with [T. E. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_of_Arabia_(film)) as I encountered some gravel in the middle of a turn.

Some people become angry with the road when that happens, because they experience cognitive dissonance and the road feels like the most likely cause for their problems. Or maybe they become angry with the government. The world is not what it ought to be, so it must logically be the government's job to fix it - it is the government's job to make sure no tiny rock gets left behind as they brush our roads clean come springtime with [plastic bristles](/psychology/stances/plastic-brushes.md) - and they have failed.

If you are one of those people, you are [delusional](/psychology/theories/illusion-of-guilt.md). The only person worth getting angry at is yourself, for driving so fast through a turn that you aren't even able to handle some gravel. You people probably didn't learn in school that cognitive dissonance is the feedback mechanism for your personal boundaries.

So no matter what you want to do, there is no point in becoming angry with the road. Roads don't feel cognitive dissonance - roads improve through different mechanisms.

Governments can feel cognitive dissonance, because governments are made up of people. But if you are the type of person who becomes angry with the government because there is some gravel in the road, the only productive thing you are likely to make those people feel is the desire to no longer work in the government. But that is okay, because it might be that you are actually the right candidate to take their place.

I don't know anything about you, but if you are the type of person who complains at the slightest inconvenience when the rest of us are obviously trying our best to make society a better place, you sound a bit like me. I always want every detail to be sorted out perfectly. But unlike you, I don't have the stamina for politics. I'm a scientist by heart, so I can only thank the politicians for their dedication and service to improving my life, because they most certainly are much better at doing so than I am.

I think that most politicians are at risk of feeling overwhelmed with the sheer magnitude of pressure that results from having to set boundaries for thousands - or even millions - of people. I know that I would feel overwhelmed. Governments are actually one of the few places where boundary setting for other people is allowed to happen, and it looks like an exhausting job.

But if you happen to be like me when it comes to complaining about details, and you are also interested in politics, you could actually be immune to such oversensitive feelings.

While most people in politics are bound to learn that there are pros and cons to every decision they make - that some people will always react negatively while others react positively every time they open their mouth - you might actually have figured out how to eliminate the cons and only implement the pros. And while most people learn that when you only want to implement the pros the whole ordeal stops abruptly as you blow up the budget by a factor of ten, you might actually be a millionaire who can fix that problem by donating the missing funds as voluntary taxes. Thus we can finally make sure there is never any gravel in the road that can kill our beloved motorcyclists. And our beloved motorcyclists can finally ride through turns at a suicidal pace without having to worry about dying.

If you are not a millionaire, fear not. Balancing budgets might also be a strong suit of yours. I prefer never having to do that, which is probably the reason I don't have a home right now - homes are simply too expensive, and I only have enough money to cover the down payment for a loan. So even though you might not be rich, I can almost guarantee that you are better at balancing budgets than I am.

If you are one of those people who are able to fuel their anger into fixing the world, reach out and let me know, and I will recommend that people vote for you for local governments. You actually have a superpower, because you are willing to fight harder than the rest of us in order to make the world around us function properly.

I might not recommend you for any national form of government, however. Because, in my experience, people who are angry might have a hard time setting boundaries for themselves. And they will sometimes be willing to do drastic things in order to avoid having to deal with their own cognitive dissonance.

Cognitive dissonance means that there is something in your [worldview](/psychology/theories/explanatory-models.md) that should be changed - that your personal boundaries are wrong. But it might be that you refuse to change anything about your beliefs or opinions, and if that is the case, you are at risk of being perceived as an asshole. I think we can all agree that the most famous democratically elected politician in the world was such an asshole, and he decided to take matters into his own hands and fix the entire world for the rest of us - setting boundaries for other people when [we didn't actually want him to do that](/media/tv-series/gutta-pa-skauen.md).

I recommend you go see a therapist instead if you need guidance on how to set personal boundaries for yourself. Fixing the world should be left to the people who only ever become angry with themselves.

Not a day goes by when I didn't wish that the most famous democratically elected politician in the world had been a close personal friend of [Sigmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Freud). But Sigmund happened to be Jewish, and that leads me to conclude that antisemitism is evil.

Sigmund also died in `September 1939`, at the age of eighty-three. How poetic.
