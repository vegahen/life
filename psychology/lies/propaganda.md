> `SSH`: Propaganda is lies

That's not true.

Many people feel confused when they look at propaganda from another country, but not when they view propaganda from their own country. That is completely natural, because the feeling you are experiencing is cognitive dissonance, and the reason you are experiencing it with other countries is that you are watching stories from a [completely different planet](/psychology/theories/planet-theory.md) from the ones you are used to.

Propaganda does sometimes contain a couple of white lies, though. But that is unavoidable, because people tend not to like thinking too much about the [actual horrors of war](/media/tv-mini-series/apocalypse-la-1ere-guerre-mondiale.md). So they typically need to provide a somewhat rose-painted image of reality in order to empathize with the target audience.

It all becomes a lot clearer if we compare what stories really are in a few different contexts, though:

| Type        | Name           | Kind    | Context      | Learning Mechanism     | Format                 |
| ----------- | -------------- | ------- | ------------ | ---------------------- | ---------------------- |
| Information | Experience     | Stories | Life         | Die Gedanken sind frei | Memories               |
| Information | Core Values    | Stories | School       | Indoctrination         | Textbooks              |
| Information | Science        | Stories | University   | Critical Thinking      | Expensive Textbooks    |
| Information | Common Decency | Stories | Prison       | Brainwashing           | Rehabilitation         |
| Information | Narratives     | Stories | News         | Pick Your Poison       | Segments, Newspapers   |
| Information | Propaganda     | Stories | Wartime News | Pick Your Poison       | Lego Movies Apparently |

This is a simplification, of course, but it should illustrate the general picture. Like even though we can't say for sure in what grade children actually learn critical thinking, we do know that critical thinking is impossible without bootstrapping the brain with some initial knowledge first.

I have tried to use the most useful definitions of the different terms.

Like **indoctrination** is learning predetermined stories without critical thinking.

**Brainwashing** is learning predetermined stories without critical thinking under threat of punishment.

**Die Gedanken sind frei** is using the creative process to come up with a bunch of different stories and then test them against reality in order to figure out which one is the best fit - [the scientific method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_method), pretty basic stuff.

And then **critical thinking** is die Gedanken sind frei but you also receive some stories from your textbooks or professors in order to make the whole thing a bit easier. Imagine if every student had to rediscover Newton's equations and Einstein's theories from scratch; we wouldn't be making much progress in science if we weren't allowed to stand on the shoulders of giants. Especially when you consider the recent developments in human intelligence.

If you want to learn some more scientific stories, I can recommend [the best show](/media/tv-mini-series/ascent-of-man.md) and the [second best show](/media/tv-mini-series/cosmos.md) ever made right here. I find that most of the stuff in your average science show can be deduplicated into those two, and they are a lot higher in quality because clickbaity storytelling, sloppy CGI, and short attention spans had not yet been invented in the seventies. So I recommend that you don't waste your time with any of that other crap - [spend some time in nature](https://www.psykologmedsovepose.no) and see for yourself instead, and bring some friends along so you can waste your time together.
