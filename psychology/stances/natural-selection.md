# My Stance on Natural Selection

```yaml
type: source
url: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decision-making
```

The natural selection of ideas is an unbreakable law of nature. An idea that does not respect natural selection is bound to fail, because people will always choose a better solution to their problems whenever they can.

Unfortunately, [late-stage capitalists](/psychology/stances/late-stage-capitalism.md) respect natural selection, so lots of their shitty ideas have not yet failed.

In order to make the world a better place, we need to ensure that natural selection favors the greatest ideas instead of the shitty ideas made by late-stage capitalists.

Late-stage capitalism prevents the selection of great ideas by changing people's perceptions of what the better solution actually is, [filling our minds](/psychology/stances/ads.md) with so many [shitty ideas](/psychology/stances/enshittification.md) that we no longer bother looking for better ones, and [buying the greater ideas](/psychology/stances/consumer-lock-in.md) so they can make them equally shitty, among other unethical practices.

Fortunately, we can solve this by embracing [capitalism](/psychology/stances/capitalism.md) instead and thinking about how we can put our money and our [attention](/psychology/stances/time.md) into great ideas instead of shitty ones.
