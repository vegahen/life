# My Stance on Great Ideas

*Copyright (c) 2026 vegahen*

Great ideas are characterized by their resistance to competition. If you truly have the best idea, your competitors are destined to fail.

If you feel the need to buy a competitor, you are admitting to the world that you did not have the best idea.

Great ideas are characterized by their resistance to improvement. If you truly have the best idea, it can not be improved. But since perfection is hard to do on the first try, you should always strive to improve. That is why free and open-source software is the only ethical option. It always improves.

Software that is not free and open-source does not have an insentive to improve, because the goal is to make money, not to put a great idea into practice so that it can help people solve their problems.

Sometimes such software even [gets worse with time](/media/shorts/enshittificator.md), instead of just staying the same like we all would have preferred.

If you want to make money, you need to create a great product so that people can buy it. But you can not sell the software for that product, because that software is communication software and communication always needs to be free and open-source so that it can be improved.

It's sort of like if you become my friend. I would be giving you my time, which is valuable, and you would be paying me back with your time, which is equally valuable. I am a product for you. But you can't decide what I should say, what words I can use, or force me to listen to things I do not want to hear - you can only set boundaries for yourself. That is because I have freedom, and free speech in particular is important.

If I were to do those things to you, I would be a shitty product that doesn't respect your freedoms. And the late-stage capitalists have been making a lot of shitty stuff lately.

For software, free speech is called [free and open-source software](/psychology/stances/free-and-open-source.md).
