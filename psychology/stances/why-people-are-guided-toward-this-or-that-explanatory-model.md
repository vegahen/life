# My Stance on Why People Are Guided Toward This or That Explanatory Model

As a child, you make up your mind about a couple of core values, such as

> `SSH`: I like my country

You are now more likely to root for your country's sports teams. You are also more likely to root for your country when it goes to war.

Core values can change later in life, and when they do, it can sometimes manifest itself as an intense experience. Should you end up with a core value such as

> `SSH`: The world can not be trusted

you might be more likely to have a difficult time making friends. Luckily, you are also more likely to become a conspiracy theorist. So you can make some conspiracy theorist friends instead.

Like any other phenomenon in the real world, we need to follow the rules of natural selection. So if you don't want someone to select specific core values, the solution is trivial. You just need to make sure that you never give them any reason to believe that such a core value is true.

For example, many people feel that

> `SSH`: I am not good enough

> `SSH`: Strangers can not be trusted

> `SSH`: War is necessary

That is probably because someone or something traumatized them at some point in their life and made them feel that way. Or because someone empathized with them at some point in their life and made them want to feel the same way they did about things, because hearing those ideas was a positive experience.
